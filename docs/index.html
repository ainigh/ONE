The cycle

A session is a rhythm you choose once.

Pick a length from the menu bar. ONE alternates Opened and Closed until it's over, and every change waits for you to press Start.

Opened · the Mac is yoursClosed · every screen coveredConfirmation · nothing moves until Start

Three lengths, nothing else

Each mode runs 4, 8 or 16 minutes. You change it on the confirmation, for the mode about to start. There's no snooze, no extend and no skip.

Reminders that halve

A pop-up in the middle of the screen tells you what's left, at halving intervals, with a sound you'll hear.

08:0004:0002:0001:00

Sessions end themselves

10 minutes to try it, or 1, 2, 4 or 8 hours. The session ends at the first confirmation after its time is up, never in the middle of a mode.

Closed

When it closes, it means it.

Every connected screen goes dark. Esc, ⌘Q, ⌘W and ⌘H go nowhere, and clicks don't fall through.

The canvas

Closed doesn't mean empty.

The blocked screen is a canvas you write on. Surfaces you can drag, resize and colour, on a space that goes on forever. It's there every time the screen closes, and one click away when it doesn't.

Write straight on it

Double-click anywhere and type. No edit mode, no save button.

Drag, resize, colour

Twelve colours and four text sizes. ⌥-drag makes a copy, and double-clicking the corner fits a surface to its text.

Never lost

Undo covers everything. If you wander off, a prompt brings you back. It's kept on your Mac, with the last good copy alongside.

Opens any time

A pencil in the menu bar opens it in a window. During Closed it fills the main screen and can't be closed.

The small things

Built to hold up on a long day.

A clock in the menu bar

The icon is a pie that empties as the mode runs out, so a glance says how far along you are.

Sounds you'll hear

Every change plays 18 dB louder than macOS would, through a limiter so it never distorts. Four loudness levels in the menu.

Survives a restart

Quit is refused mid-session. If the Mac restarts, ONE opens at login and goes straight back to a confirmation.

Every screen

Plug in a display mid-block and it's covered within a moment. The main screen keeps the canvas.

Sleep-proof

Every countdown runs from a deadline, not a tick. Close the lid mid-mode and it picks up exactly where it should.

Keeps itself current

Between sessions it checks for a new version, downloads it, or builds it on your Mac if GitHub hasn't yet.

Install

One line in Terminal.

It puts ONE in your Applications folder and opens it. Running the line again is always safe.

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ainigh/ONE/main/install.sh | bash
  1. Step 1

    Start a session

    Click the ring in the menu bar and pick a length. Try 10 minutes first, with 4-minute slots.

  2. Step 2

    Press Start

    Every mode change asks first. Return starts, and ← → pick the length.

  3. Step 3

    Let it close

    When the screen goes dark, write on the canvas, or just stop for a few minutes.

Can I get out of Closed?

Once an hour, by solving the puzzle, which buys a 20-minute pause. ONE is friction, not a prison: Force Quit and the power button still work, because macOS reserves them. The point is that the easy ways out are gone.

What happens to my notes?

They stay on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/CountdownOverlay/canvas.json, with the previous copy kept beside it. Nothing is sent anywhere.

What if the Mac sleeps or restarts mid-session?

Countdowns run from deadlines, so sleep doesn't stretch them. After a restart, ONE opens at login and goes back to a confirmation for the mode that was interrupted, with its full length.

Does it update itself?

Yes, between sessions. It checks at launch and every six hours, and offers the update in its menu.
